# Meeting Prep — 1 Hour with Renuka

## The one principle for this meeting

**Do not spend the hour on anything that is already in the playbook.** You have
read it. The differentiated value of a live hour with someone who runs a working
system is her **tacit knowledge**: what broke, what decayed, what she abandoned,
what she'd do differently, and *seeing it run live*. Spend the hour there.

## The single most important framing (internalize before you walk in)

Her system is **proven on Claude Code, not Hermes.** She has **not** wired Obsidian
into Hermes — by her own admission that is her *next* build. Your plan centers
**Hermes as chief of staff.** Implications:

- **Learn from her on:** the vault/second-brain architecture, taxonomy, the
  five-layer memory model, the daily skill rhythm, and the calibrated-thought-partner
  layer. These are **tool-neutral** and port to Hermes directly.
- **Do NOT expect answers from her on:** the Hermes wiring itself (file access,
  loading rules every session, saved-workflow mechanics, scheduler). She hasn't
  done it. This is where *you two are peers* — possibly where you can become build
  partners, or where you get ahead.
- **A real opening:** offer to be her Hermes-integration test case. You may give
  as much as you get.

---

## Your positions going in (be clear about these — they shape every question)

State these early so she can react to *your* design, not a generic one:

1. **You want personal + professional INTEGRATED.** She runs **hard walls**
   (isolated vaults per context). This is the biggest divergence. Decide whether
   to adopt walls, integrate, or do a hybrid — *after* hearing her reasoning.
2. **Hermes is your chief of staff**, orchestrating Claude Code + Codex. Her model
   is mostly one human-orchestrated agent. You want more orchestration/automation.
3. **Your memory layer is Mem0 + Honcho + Hermes.** Find out what she *actually*
   runs vs. just names in the deck.
4. **Files / tools / schema are open.** You came to learn her schema before
   committing to your own.

## The grill list (prioritized — you only have 60 minutes)

### TIER 1 — only she can answer; highest value (spend the most time here)

1. **Walls vs integration.** "I want personal and professional *integrated* in one
   brain. You went the opposite way — hard isolated walls. *Why?* What concretely
   broke or bled when contexts shared a vault? What would I lose if I integrate?"
   - Follow-up: How do you handle a query that spans two walls? Does anything ever
     legitimately need to live in two places?
2. **The maintenance reality.** "What's the *actual* upkeep cost? You say session-end
   is the keystone — what happens the weeks you skip it? What decays fastest?"
   - This is the failure mode that kills these systems. Get the honest number.
3. **What you'd do differently.** "If you rebuilt today from zero, what would you
   *not* do again? What did you build, use for a week, and abandon?"
4. **The calibration layer — was it worth it?** "How long from starting the
   behavioral log until the thought-partner behavior actually paid off? Would you
   tell me to do it now, or wait until the basics run?"
   - Follow-up: How much of your time does the calibration upkeep actually take?
5. **Multi-agent — have you really done 'one agent raises another'?** "Is the
   second-agent supervision real and running, or still aspirational? If real: show
   me. If not: what's the blocker?"

### TIER 2 — learn from her working system (ask her to SHOW, not tell)

6. **Live demo (ask for screen share):**
   - A real `good morning` / morning standup.
   - A real `capture:` routing in flight.
   - Her actual `MEMORY.md` and one `_Vault-Bridge.md`.
   - Her `.claude/skills/` folder — which ones are *actually* used daily.
7. **The taxonomy gaps.** "Why `00, 01, 02, 04, 05, 09, 12` — what happened to 03,
   06, 07, 08, 10, 11? What did you add or kill over time?" (The gaps imply she
   pruned. Learn what she removed and why.)
8. **The memory tools, for real.** "Do you actually run Mem0 / Honcho / G-Brain
   today, or is the vault doing all the work? Where's the real line between the
   conversation-memory layer and the vault in practice?"
   - Directly relevant: your plan leans on Mem0 + Honcho. Find out if she validated them.
9. **Skills that earned their place.** "Of all the skills, which 5 do you actually
   invoke daily? Which did you write and never use?"

### TIER 3 — your specific path (last 10-15 min)

10. **Hermes as chief of staff.** "I want Hermes orchestrating Claude Code + Codex.
    You've done the Claude Code half. Where do you see the integration being hard?
    What in your setup assumes Claude Code specifically and won't port cleanly?"
11. **Codex's role.** "You don't use Codex. Any reason it wouldn't slot in as a
    second builder alongside Claude Code under Hermes?"
12. **What to NOT build.** "What's the trap — the thing that looks worth building
    but is actually a way of avoiding the work?" (Her README literally warns about this.)

## Decisions you want to walk OUT with (even tentatively)

1. **Walls vs integrated vs hybrid** — at least a lean, with her reasoning on record.
2. **Whether to adopt her taxonomy as-is** or modify it for an integrated brain.
3. **Build the calibration layer now or later** — based on her payoff timeline.
4. **A realistic maintenance budget** you're actually willing to pay (or you don't
   build the layers that need it).

## Ask her to send you afterward

- A **redacted copy of her vault skeleton + MEMORY.md + one real skill** (you have
  the genericized playbook versions; her real ones show the deltas).
- Her **`CLAUDE.md`** (sanitized) — the global rules that actually matter.
- Any **notes on what she's learned about wiring Hermes**, even fragments.

## Watch-outs (don't let the hour drift here)

- Re-explaining the playbook to each other. You both read it. Skip it.
- Tool evangelism about Mem0/Honcho/etc. in the abstract. Tie every memory-tool
  question to "do you run it, did it earn its place."
- Designing your whole schema live. The schema is open *on purpose* — the goal
  tomorrow is to learn enough to design it *after*, not during.
